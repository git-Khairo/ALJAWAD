# AlJawad Trading — Product Context

**register:** brand (public site), product (client and admin dashboards)

The public site is where design *is* the product: it has to earn trust before
anyone signs up. The dashboards are where design *serves* the product: they
carry real money data and must get out of the way.

## What this is

الجواد للتداول / AlJawad Trading. A Syrian trading firm, based in Damascus
(Abu Rumana), founded by working traders. It sells six things:

1. Daily technical analysis
2. VIP trade tracking (clients watch the desk trade in real time)
3. Education, beginner through professional
4. Local deposit and withdrawal rails that actually work inside Syria
5. Support, around the clock
6. One-to-one and group coaching

It is not a broker and it is not an exchange. It is an analysis, education and
access business, which means the product being sold is **judgement**. Every
design decision should ask whether it makes the firm's judgement look more
credible or less.

## Users

**Primary: the Syrian retail trader.** Twenties to forties. Reads Arabic first,
English second. Checks the site on a mid-range Android phone, on cellular, in
daylight. Often on a slow or metered connection. Has almost certainly been
burned before, either by a scam signal group or by a broker that could not
process a withdrawal.

**Secondary: the diaspora client.** Outside Syria, sending money in or trading
alongside family. More likely on desktop, more likely reading English.

**Tertiary: the desk itself.** Coaches and admins living in the dashboards all
day, doing CRM, finance, scheduling and content work.

## The one thing

> Syrians who want to trade have no way to tell an honest desk from a scam.

The company's own words for its founding: *بيئة تداول نزيهة وشفافة بعيدة عن
الضبابية والتضليل*. An honest, transparent trading environment, free of
obfuscation and misleading practice.

That sentence is the design brief. Every visual choice either supports it or
undermines it.

## Tone

Plain, specific, unhurried. Numbers stated exactly, never rounded up for
effect. Arabic is the primary voice and should read as written by a person,
not translated by a tool. English is a genuine second language on the site,
not an afterthought.

Never: hype, urgency, countdown pressure, profit screenshots, guarantees,
"unlock", "elevate", "seamless", "next-gen". A firm selling honesty cannot use
the vocabulary of the people selling dishonesty.

## Anti-references

These are the things AlJawad must not be mistaken for.

- **The signal-group aesthetic.** Glowing candlestick charts, Lamborghinis,
  profit screenshots, green arrows everywhere. This is what every scam in the
  region looks like.
- **Crypto-casino dark mode.** Glassmorphism, neon glow, animated grids,
  particle fields, gradient headlines. The previous build of this site was
  exactly this.
- **Generic SaaS.** Rounded cards on a gradient, three columns of
  icon-plus-heading, a glowing CTA.

The brand's teal and navy stay. That makes the discipline of everything else
the only thing separating this site from the first two entries on that list,
since they use the same colours. Restraint is doing the work here, not
palette.

## Strategic principles

1. **Trust is legibility.** Anything hidden behind decoration reads as
   something being hidden. Prefer plain statement over reveal-on-scroll.
2. **Arabic first, structurally.** RTL is the default direction, not a mirror
   mode bolted on. Type, rhythm and asymmetry all have to work in both.
3. **The tape is the truth.** Live market data is the one piece of the site
   that cannot be faked. Give it real estate and set it honestly, including
   when markets are closed and nothing is moving.
4. **The horse is the only ornament.** الجواد is the noble steed. One strong
   mark, used sparingly, beats a page of effects.
5. **Weigh the phone first.** A heavy hero costs a Syrian user real money in
   data. Decoration has a budget and the budget is small.
6. **Say the risk.** CFD trading loses most retail traders money. Stating that
   plainly is both a legal requirement and the single most credible thing on
   the page.
